THE ONE MESSAGE

This governs everything below it.

You either have a trap or a plan. The trap is staying in the old economy. The plan is moving to the new economy. There are two options, nothing in between.

The opening frame, in the Method's words: you built the business by saying yes to everything. That was the right call early on. Staying there is the trap.

Old economy (the trap): sell licences, implement by the hour, serve every vertical, take every lead, and sit and pray AI does not reach your revenue.

New economy (the plan): keep the profitable parts of the old. Add your own product, custom builds, or consulting. Market to one vertical. Build a system so the business does not run through you.

The reassurance that makes the message land: you do not drop what you do today, and you do not need to become a complete business consultant. You add to what you already sell. The partner who broadens the offer keeps the seat at the client's table, and wins whichever way AI plays out.

The pressure, stated with restraint: AI is absorbing some of the implementation, integration, and setup work, and vendors are tightening licence terms. Not all of it, and not overnight. Content never claims AI is killing implementation. Doom reads as hype and the reader tunes out. The honest version is stronger: nobody knows how far it goes, and the partner who adds something wins either way.

The domino is the vertical decision. Every other decision depends on it. Content hammers this one message until Paul is bored of it. Three months minimum from 8 October 2026. No additions.

THE THREE DECISIONS

WHO, WHAT, HOW. In this order. Every piece of core content leads to one of them.

WHO: the vertical. Which vertical do you market to? Three tests: where your best results came from, whether the vertical is on a rising tide, whether you already have the network and the language. A marketing decision, not a delivery restriction. Run as a 90-day test with a clear stop-or-commit point. Until the vertical is chosen, nothing else compounds.

WHAT: the offer. Two parts. The platform: one or several, decided by what the vertical's buyers already run. What you add: one of three, picked first.

Own product. A vertical solution built on top of the platform, vibe coded once, sold as a package or monthly licence to every client in the vertical. Slowest to revenue, least owner time in delivery.

Custom build. Bespoke to one client, roughly 70% platform, 30% vibe coded for the gaps. Fixed scope. Fastest to revenue.

Consulting. Architecture and design before anyone builds, priced above implementation, including AI strategy across the client's business. Outcome or retainer. Fast to revenue, most owner time.

Licences and implementation stay. The add sits on top.

HOW: the system. Two parts, in order.

Part 1, create the capacity. Build agents and skills in AI that do the repeatable work. Make the people you already have more effective with AI before hiring more.

Part 2, own your lead generation. Three channels: inbound (content), outbound (LinkedIn outreach and events aimed at the vertical), partners (existing vendor, parallel vendors, people who already have your audience). When cash is tight, start with a reactivation program to past clients.

Content that argues generally about "focus" or "positioning" without landing on one of these three decisions is off-message. Send it back.

The loop is a storytelling structure that lives inside every piece of content, regardless of format. Three beats.

Beat 1 — Name the trap. Vertical confusion first: too many verticals, invisible positioning, AI eating the implementation layer, rented-seat dependence on the platform. Referral drought and certification wallpaper are symptoms of it. Golf angle: obsessing over technique while the score does not move.

A solo podcast episode runs all three beats across 15 minutes. The trap gets a full story. The consequence gets real numbers. The example carries start to finish.

A newsletter issue runs all three beats with one named character carrying the reader through.

The campaign email is always Beat 3 at scale.

PROOF STANDARD

Named client outcomes: recorded proof required. Fathom transcript exists. Fictional or real name, platform, specific numbers, timeframe. Results only, never method.

Principles and patterns: no recorded proof required. World examples, golf analogies, and the Coca-Cola bottler parallel are legitimate for universal truths. VOICE STANDARD

Voice style is governed by the PHM Voice Style skill in Claude (BREN project). Apply before publishing. Unsure it sounds like Paul: run pub-test.

Short version: short sentences, one idea per line, no em-dashes, no banned phrases, names and numbers always specific, North American Neutral, never open with "I" or the reader's name, links in comments not post body, at least one platform named early, a person in every story.

HOOKS

The hook is the most important line in any piece. Formula and examples in the hook-generator skill. Run it for every piece.

The standard is the banger first line: a curiosity gap the reader has to close. "A former business partner of mine just cut 30% of his workforce" forces the next line. The hook opens a loop, it does not close one. When using hook-generator, add: "Write as an open loop. Do not answer the question the hook raises."

Preferred v4.0 hook families: rented-seat reframes, AI-erosion beats, one-vertical-versus-five, David-versus-Goliath moments.
