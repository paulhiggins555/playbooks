PHM CASE STUDY BUILDER PLAYBOOK

Turn the client results already on your call recordings into proof that sells.

Multiplier Method: WHO (Marketing funnel and proof) | Paul Higgins Mentoring | October 2026

Overview

This playbook turns the client wins sitting in your head and your call recordings into verified case studies you can use on LinkedIn, in your newsletter and inside sales conversations.

You have clients getting real results. A Zoho rollout that cut quoting time in half. A NetSuite migration that let the finance team close the month in three days instead of ten. Ask you about it and you can tell the story. Look at your website and LinkedIn and there is no trace of it. The result exists in exactly one place: your memory.

That costs you every time a prospect asks if you have done this before. I have seen this pattern across 200+ SaaS partners. The ones who get inbound talk about results in numbers. The ones who say "we improved their processes" compete on price.

This idea builds on work from my mentor, James Schramko, who mines his existing call recordings for client stories instead of booking new interviews. I have combined it with the PHM proof standard. The PHM angle is your WHO. Every story you bank should prove the promise you make to the clients you want more of.

A result you cannot describe in one sentence is a result you cannot sell with. This fixes that.

Pre-Flight Checklist

☐Access to your call recordings and transcripts: Fathom, Zoom, Teams or Gong.

☐A list of 5 to 10 current or past clients who achieved a result you could measure.

☐Your one-line WHO and promise written down, so you know which stories matter most.

☐A simple story bank: one sheet with client, platform, result, proof tag, permission status and the offer it proves.

☐A clause in your client agreement allowing you to reference outcomes, with the client approving the wording first.

☐A clear rule on proof: RECORDED means it is in a transcript. RECALLED means you are working from memory.

Step-by-Step Process

1 Sweep your recordings three times

What you are doing

Going through your existing call transcripts looking for one thing at a time, instead of reading every call for everything.

Why this works

Transcripts capture the client while they are solving the problem, not trying to sound polished for a camera two months later. One pass looking for everything flattens every story into the same shape. Three focused passes catch what one long read misses.

How to execute

1.First pass: look for the arc. A problem the client recognised, a turning point and a result. Flag any call that has all three.

2.Second pass: look for the number. Revenue, hours saved, days to close the month, deals won.

3.Third pass: look for the line. The sentence where the client says it better than you ever would. Copy it word for word.

4.Note the transcript reference and timestamp against every story you find.

Common mistake

•Booking a new interview before checking what you already have. The story is usually already on tape.

2 Grade what you find and tag the proof

What you are doing

Sorting stories by how ready they are, and marking every claim by where it came from.

Why this works

Not every story is ready to publish. James Schramko grades stories into three tiers: a full arc with a hard number is ready now, a clear problem and turning point with a soft result is held for later, and a documented stall with nothing resolved yet is filed as an early story. The PHM proof standard sits on top. A RECORDED claim came from a transcript. A RECALLED claim came from memory, and it never gets a client name or a number attached until the client confirms it in writing.

How to execute

1.Mark each story A (ready), B (needs an ending) or C (too early).

2.Tag every claim RECORDED or RECALLED.

3.For B stories, plan to ask the client one question on your next call: what is the most concrete change you have seen since we started?

4.Anything untagged goes back to the transcript. Treat it as RECALLED at best.

Common mistake

•Rounding a vague result up into a specific one. "They grew" only becomes "they added $80,000 in annual revenue" if the transcript says $80,000.

3 Shape each story in four parts

What you are doing

Writing every A-grade story as Before, Shift, After and Pattern. Nothing extra, nothing missing.

Why this works

Buyers take in proof in that order. They need to recognise the Before, believe the Shift, want the After and take away the Pattern. Skip a part and the story loses its pull. Write the After first. Starting with the result forces you to be specific.

How to execute

1.After: the most specific number or outcome, stated first.

2.Before: the client's situation in two or three sentences, in their language where you have it.

3.Shift: the one decision or change that turned it. Not a list of everything you did.

4.Pattern: the principle a prospect in the same situation can take away, stated plainly.

Common mistake

•Giving away the method in the Shift. Describe what changed, not a step-by-step of how to do it. The how is what clients pay you for.

Pro tip: Write the Pattern as a direct statement tied to your WHO. A pattern that applies to everyone proves nothing. A pattern that names the exact situation your ideal client is in does the selling for you.

4 Map every story to what you sell

What you are doing

Tagging each story with the offer or promise it proves before you file it.

Why this works

A pile of good stories is not yet a marketing asset. A story about a client who finally trusted their pipeline report proves a different offer than one about a client who stopped re-keying orders. Tag it now or you will find it in six months and wonder why you kept it.

How to execute

1.Next to each story, write the offer it proves and the ideal client it speaks to.

2.Rank your A stories by strength of number, clarity of arc and how directly they prove your main promise.

3.Work from the top ten. Leave the rest in the bank for later.

Common mistake

•Publishing every story at once. A proof bank is worth more in reserve, ready for the prospect it fits.

5 Clear permission and anonymise

What you are doing

Getting the client's approval for named use, or stripping out anything that identifies them.

Why this works

Publishing a result because the client seemed happy on a call puts the relationship and your reputation at risk. Seeming happy and giving permission are different things. Where you cannot get a yes, the story still works without the name. The arc and the result do the work, not the logo.

How to execute

1.Ask the client, on whatever channel they reply to fastest, if you can share the result. Offer to send the exact wording first.

2.If they hesitate, offer anonymous use: role, industry and platform instead of name.

3.For anonymous stories, remove details that only an insider would recognise, and round numbers only where they could identify the client.

4.Record the permission status in your story bank.

Common mistake

•Treating a verbal yes as permanent. Confirm what was agreed in one line, in writing, the same day.

6 Use one story three ways

What you are doing

Turning each cleared story into a short post, a newsletter piece and a full case study.

Why this works

One extraction can do three different jobs. A short post earns attention. A newsletter piece builds trust over time. A full case study closes the belief gap when a prospect is deciding. James Schramko documented an estimating service for trade businesses that went from about $27,000 profit in its first part year to $370,000 eighteen months later. That story did not need a special interview. It built up call by call while the business changed.

How to execute

1.Short post: open with the After, give the Before in two lines, close with the Pattern.

2.Newsletter: the same story with more of the Before and why the Shift mattered.

3.Full case study: all four parts plus what the client did first and what they would tell a peer.

4.Save all three next to the story in your bank, so you can find it in a sales conversation within a minute.

Common mistake

•Using a story once and never again. The bank only pays if you go back to it.

How the Result Question Flows

No scripts. You know your clients better than anyone. Use this structure at the end of client calls and say it your way. Over time it fills your recordings with RECORDED proof.

1.Ask for the most concrete change. What has actually moved since you started working together.

2.Push gently for a number. If the answer is vague, ask for the closest they can get to a figure.

3.Ask what it was like before. Their words for the Before are worth more than yours.

4.Ask what made the difference. That is your Shift, in their language.

5.Ask if you can share it. Offer to send the wording first, or to keep it anonymous.

The test: could you write the After in one sentence with a number in it, using only what is on the recording? If not, the story is not ready yet.

Next Steps

1.Today: name one client win you already know about and write the one-sentence After.

2.This week: run the three-pass sweep on your last ten client calls and grade what you find.

3.By end of Week 2: one A story written in four parts, tagged, mapped to an offer and cleared with the client.

4.By end of Month 1: that story used three ways, at least three entries in your story bank, and the result question on the end of every client call.

The result sitting in your head is worth nothing. The same result, verified and published, wins your next client.
