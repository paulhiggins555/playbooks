Newsletter Playbook

The seven-section weekly newsletter that turns readers who are not ready to book into replies and booked calls.

By Paul Higgins · Updated June 2026

Purpose

The newsletter is your nurture channel. It turns readers who are not ready to book into readers who eventually reply and do. Every issue exists to produce one of two outcomes: a reply from a qualified prospect, or a booked call. Not opens. Not clicks. Replies and bookings.

Who you are writing for

Write for one reader: the owner of an established, founder-led business that fits your ideal client profile. Name it precisely once and keep it fixed: the platform or industry they work in, their revenue range [e.g. $300K to $1M], team size [e.g. 1 to 10], region, and years in operation.

Most owners at this stage show up with one of three problems:

  • Who: serving everyone, no clear niche, inconsistent revenue.
  • What: half recurring, half project work, dependent on referrals or one lead source.
  • How: buried in delivery, no time for outreach, still the last set of eyes on everything.

Decide who you are not writing for as well, for example solo operators, people who just left a corporate job, or anyone who wants validation rather than decisions.

The three decisions

Every issue sits inside one of three decisions. Name the decision before you write. Never explain your full method. Show the result, name the pattern, and leave the gap a call fills.

  • Who you serve. When the market is clear, marketing sharpens, pricing strengthens, and the right clients find you. Until it is clear, nothing else compounds.
  • What you sell. A second pillar on top of existing work, such as a productized service or an AI capability your platform cannot deliver. Something that earns without the owner in every delivery.
  • How the business runs. First create capacity through documented processes and AI delegation. Then own lead generation: outreach first, then strategic partners, then content.

The newsletter structure

Every issue has seven sections in this order. The sequence does not change.

  1. Personal opening. Two to three sentences, written by the owner, about something that happened this week. It is the human entry point and builds connection before any content. Never AI generated.
  2. Three bullet points. A preview of the three main pieces of value in the issue, pulled directly from what follows. Specific, not generic. It gives the reader a reason to keep going.
  3. Authority content. One insight or story from a podcast guest, interview or group call, tied commercially to your reader's world: why it matters to them right now. Use the guest's own words where possible. The source must be a transcript.
  4. AI section. Written by the owner. Focus on AI for revenue generation, meaning marketing and sales, not delivery and operations. Draw from your own experience and what you read and watched that week. Never AI generated.
  5. Client example. One client, first name, business type and specific numbers. Show the situation before the decision, the decision made, and what happened next. The source must be a recorded transcript, checked against your proof register. Tease the solution, never teach it.
  6. What you missed. Pulled from comments and questions on last week's social posts. It invites the reader to take part and ties the newsletter to the conversation already happening.
  7. Call to action. One call, no framework, a straight read on where the real constraint is. "Book here:" plus your booking link. This block never changes.

The proof standard

Keep client proof in one register. Before using any client story, check it for recorded status, numbers and approved use. Never use recalled proof or assume a story was recorded without checking. No recorded proof means no client example that week. Results only, never the method.

The content loop

The newsletter does not work in isolation. The authority content should reinforce the week's mid-week social post theme where possible, and the client example should reinforce the end-of-week client post. The reader sees the theme on social media, then goes deeper in the newsletter. Whoever builds the weekly content plan coordinates this.

The subject line

Take the subject line from the client example or the authority content, whichever is stronger that week. A specific result or pattern. No generic claims. No em-dashes. The subject line earns the open the same way a social hook earns the click on "see more".

Test: would your ideal client stop scrolling their inbox for this line?

Ownership by section

  • The owner writes: the personal opening and the AI section, delivered by a fixed day each week.
  • A team member drafts: the bullet preview, authority content, client example and what you missed, ready for the owner's review on the same day.

Voice rules

Short sentences. One idea per line where it adds emphasis. No em-dashes. No AI padding phrases such as game-changing, unlock, transform, leverage, in today's landscape, insights or learnings. No fake compliments. Specific over general: names, numbers, timeframes. Warm but commercially firm. Results, not method. First person, always.

What success looks like

Replies from qualified prospects who recognise themselves in the client example. Booked calls from readers who have been on the list for weeks or months. A rising open rate from a smaller but more qualified list. The newsletter is not measured on subscriber count. It is measured on conversations started.

Document ownership

The owner sets the strategy and writes two sections each week. A team member analyses performance, keeps the execution SOP, and drafts the other four sections. Review this document when strategy changes, not when execution details change. Execution details live in the SOP.
