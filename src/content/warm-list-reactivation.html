Overview

This playbook turns the client list sitting in your invoicing software into your fastest source of new revenue this month.

Most of your sales energy probably goes to people who have never heard of you. LinkedIn campaigns, platform directories, a new website, a webinar. Meanwhile every client who already trusted you with a project sits in Xero, QuickBooks or an old inbox, untouched since the final invoice.

A past client already knows what you do, already believes you can do it, and already cleared the hardest step: paying you the first time. They left because the project finished, the budget moved or life got busy. Very few left because you let them down. They will not reach out first, because from where they sit, you are the busy one.

This idea comes from my mentor, James Schramko, who gives it as the first assignment to founders who join his program. I have adapted it for SaaS partners (e.g. Salesforce, Zoho, Microsoft, Monday, NetSuite, Acumatica), with one addition: the list you build here is also the best evidence you will ever have for your WHO decision.

The warm list answers faster and pays sooner than any new audience. Work it first.

Pre-Flight Checklist

  • Login to your invoicing software (Xero, QuickBooks, FreshBooks) with permission to export the full customer list, not just this year.
  • Access to your CRM, even the one you stopped updating, plus your platform partner portal for past deal registrations.
  • Your inbox ready to search: "invoice", "proposal", "SOW", "quote".
  • A blank spreadsheet: name, company, platform, industry, last project, liked, paid on time, best channel, outcome.
  • Two 90-minute blocks in your calendar this week for reaching out, marked as client time.
  • A one-line answer to "what are you doing now?" that sounds like you.
  • A decision made, in advance, that nobody on this list gets a discount.

Step-by-Step Process

Step 1: Count what you sent last month

What you are doing

Measuring the sequencing error before you fix it.

Why this works

Most partners I speak with have never counted. Once the number is in front of them, the decision makes itself.

How to execute

  1. Write down every new-audience activity from the last 30 days and roughly what it cost you in hours and dollars.
  2. Count every message you sent to someone who has paid you, with no invoice attached.
  3. Look at your last 16 clients and mark where each came from. Count how many came from people who already knew you.

Common mistake

  • Counting project update emails to current clients. This is about people who are not currently buying.

Step 2: Build the full list in one afternoon

What you are doing

Putting everyone who has ever paid you, plus every serious enquiry that went quiet, into one spreadsheet.

Why this works

You cannot work a list that does not exist. Most founders have never built this, so their best prospects live in five different systems and none of them gets contacted.

How to execute

  1. Export every customer from your invoicing software, all years.
  2. Pull every closed-won and closed-lost deal from your CRM and partner portal.
  3. Search your inbox for invoices, proposals and quotes. Add anyone missing.
  4. Add serious enquiries: discovery calls that went quiet, proposals that stalled.

Common mistake

  • Filtering while you build. Get every name in first. Ranking comes next.

Pro tip

When a contact has moved companies, list both the old company and the new one. They may need you in both places.

Step 3: Rank by liked and paid on time

What you are doing

Choosing who you invite back. You get to choose.

Why this works

Two questions sort the list better than any scoring model. Did you like working with them? Did they pay on time? Yes to both goes to the top.

How to execute

  1. For every paying client, mark Liked (Y/N) and Paid on time (Y/N). For enquiries who never paid, mark only Liked.
  2. Sort: paid clients scoring Y/Y first, then paid clients with one Y, then liked enquiries.
  3. Pick your top 10.
  4. Look at the Y/Y group by industry. Write down which industry appears most.

Pro tip

That last step is your WHO decision hiding in plain sight. When your best clients cluster in one industry, that is evidence from your own records of where your best results come from. Bring the list to your next session.

Step 4: Reach the top 10 the way they like to hear from you

What you are doing

Making personal contact with the ten people at the top of your list, with no offer attached.

Why this works

You know how each of these people likes to communicate. Some pick up the phone. Some live in WhatsApp. Some respond to a quick video, some to an email. Use whatever they actually reply to. James Schramko documented a sales trainer he coaches who stopped working on his website and podcast for one week and contacted past clients and old enquiries instead. That week produced two or three likely new clients.

How to execute

  1. Next to each name, write the channel they reply to fastest: phone, WhatsApp, text, video message, email or LinkedIn.
  2. Reach out on that channel, following the conversation flow in the next section.
  3. Ask what they are working on now and listen. Most will tell you what they need before you ask.
  4. If a need comes up, book a proper conversation within 7 days rather than solving it on the spot.
  5. Note the outcome before moving to the next name.

Common mistake

  • Turning it into a pitch. The first contact is about them. The opportunity follows.

Step 5: One personal message to the rest

What you are doing

Reaching everyone below the top 10, one person at a time, on the channel each one uses.

Why this works

The list is the asset. James Schramko reported an email marketing consultant whose single campaign to a list he already had produced four sales at $6,000 each, from people who had simply not been asked.

How to execute

  1. Write each message yourself, in the words you would use with that person.
  2. Send from your own inbox, phone or account, not a newsletter or automation tool.
  3. Send 10 to 15 a day so you can answer every reply the same day.

Common mistake

  • Sending a discount, a newsletter or a launch sequence. A past client who paid full price and gets 20 percent off learns they overpaid last time.

Step 6: Reply the same day

What you are doing

Treating every reply as a live opportunity.

Why this works

The speed of your reply is the offer. James Schramko describes a retired past client who emailed at 7:30 one evening about a new business. James replied just after 11pm, and the client paid 38 minutes later.

How to execute

  1. Answer every reply the day it arrives, even if it is two lines.
  2. When someone is warm, ask who else they know running into the same thing.

Pro tip

Once a client reactivates, check in every 90 days so you never need to reactivate them again.

How the Conversation Flows

No scripts. You know your clients better than anyone, and a past client can spot a template in one line. Use this structure and say it your way, on whatever channel they prefer.

  1. Why you are reaching out. They came to mind. Mention one real thing you remember: the project, a person on their team, something they told you.
  2. Them, not you. Ask how the business is going and what they are working on now.
  3. Listen. Let them talk. Most will name a problem, a new project or someone who needs help.
  4. One line on what you do now, only if they ask.
  5. A next step if it fits. If a need came up, suggest a proper conversation. If not, leave it warm and move on.

The test: read it back before you send. If it sounds like something you would say to them over coffee, send it. If it sounds like marketing, start again.

Next Steps

  1. Today: export every client who has ever paid you. Contact the first name you would put on the list before you close the laptop.
  2. This week: build and rank the full list.
  3. By end of Week 1: your top 10 contacted on their preferred channel.
  4. By end of Month 1: everyone else contacted, every reply answered same day, and your best-client industry ready for your next session.
