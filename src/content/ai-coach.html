How to Build Your Claude Operating System

Follow these five steps in order. Each one builds on the last. Do not skip to Skills before you have set up your Profile and your first Project. The sequence matters.

Step 1: Set Your Profile Preferences

What you’re doing

Write a clear, specific description of who you are, how you work, and what you need from Claude. This loads at the start of every conversation.

Why this works

Claude has no memory between sessions by default. Profile Preferences change that. I have updated mine over 30 times. It gets sharper every iteration.

How to execute

  1. Go to Settings > Profile > find the preferences field.
  2. Write 300-500 words: your role and business, your clients, communication preferences, Always Do / Never Do.
  3. Test it: open a new chat, ask Claude to introduce itself to one of your clients. If generic, refine.
  4. Save a copy in a Google Doc. You will update this often.

Common mistake

Writing it like a CV. Claude does not need your backstory. It needs instructions. Maximum 500 words. Every line should change how Claude responds. If removing it would change nothing, cut it.

Pro tip

The most impactful lines are the 'Never do' ones. Eliminating default annoying behaviours (never start with 'Great question!', never use bullet points when I ask for prose) works faster than any positive instruction.

Step 2: Configure Your Style

What you’re doing

Create a custom style that matches your voice and communication format. Separate from preferences — this controls how Claude presents information, not what it knows about you.

How to execute

  1. Go to Settings > Styles > Create new style.
  2. Option A: Upload 3-5 examples of your best writing. Claude analyses and generates a matching style.
  3. Option B: Describe your style in text (e.g., 'Short sentences. Commercially direct. Peer-to-peer tone.').
  4. Name it clearly. Set as default or select at start of relevant conversations.
  5. Test with a real task — draft a follow-up email. Compare with and without the style applied.

Pro tip

Build a LinkedIn-specific style separate from your email style. Hook-driven short lines vs. measured structured proposals. Switching takes 5 seconds.

Step 3: Build Your First Project: The AI Business Coach

What you’re doing

Create a dedicated Claude Project that acts as your AI business advisor — loaded with context about your business, your clients, and your methodology. The equivalent of hiring a senior strategist who has read every document in your business and knows exactly where you get in your own way.

But here is what most people miss: an AI business coach is only as good as the rules you give it. Most people give Claude context and skip the accountability layer. Context without accountability gives you a smart assistant. The accountability layer is what makes it a coach.

The Three Layers of an Effective AI Business Coach

Layer 1: Context

What Claude needs to know about you and your business to give relevant advice. Your niche, your offer, your clients, your methodology. Without this, Claude gives generic answers.

Layer 2: Accountability Rules

The specific behaviours you want Claude to flag and challenge. A good AI coach does not just answer your questions. It notices when you are answering the wrong question, avoiding a decision, or doing work that belongs to someone else on your team.

Layer 3: Proof Standards

What counts as evidence in your business. If you let your AI coach accept vague claims, it will reflect your own blind spots back at you instead of challenging them.

How to Build the Accountability Layer

Before you write your Project Instructions, answer these four questions. Your answers become the rules your AI coach enforces.

Question 1: What is the sequence of decisions in your methodology?

In my work with tech business owners, the order is: WHO (positioning), then WHAT (offer), then HOW (systems and lead generation). Trying to fix HOW before WHO is confirmed is one of the most common reasons businesses stall. Your AI coach should know your sequence — and flag when you skip ahead.

Write it down

"The right decision sequence in my business is [Step 1] → [Step 2] → [Step 3]. If I ask about a later step before an earlier one is confirmed, flag it."

Question 2: Where do you personally get stuck?

Every business owner has 3-5 recurring patterns that cost them time and revenue. Common ones: explaining methodology before the prospect has committed (free consulting), building systems instead of running sales conversations, adding complexity when the constraint is actually positioning.

Write it down

"My personal stuck patterns are: [Pattern 1], [Pattern 2], [Pattern 3]. If you detect any of these in my conversation, stop and flag it before we continue."

Question 3: What is your proof standard?

An AI coach that accepts 'I think this worked' as evidence is useless. Set a specific standard. Mine requires named clients, specific numbers, and a timeframe. Without that standard, you end up with an AI that validates your assumptions instead of testing them.

Write it down

"When I make a claim about client results, confirm: Is there a named client? Are there specific numbers? Is there a timeframe? If any are missing, ask before we continue."

Question 4: What is your AI coach authorised to do unilaterally?

There are things a good coach should always do without being asked — and things it should never do regardless of how you frame the request. Define both clearly.

Write it down

"Always [list 3-5 behaviours you want without being asked]. Never [list 3-5 behaviours you want eliminated regardless of context]."

How to execute

  1. Go to claude.ai > Projects > New Project.
  2. Name it clearly: 'Business Coach' or your business name.
  3. Write Project Instructions (600-1,000 words). Use the four questions as your structure.
  4. Upload core knowledge files: niche description, offer stack, 3-5 call transcripts, any frameworks you use.
  5. Test with a real question. Claude should give specific, context-aware advice and flag if you skip a step.
  6. If the response is generic, the fix is almost always Layer 2 — not more context documents.

Real example

A consultant I work with kept giving away strategy before the prospect had committed. His AI coach instructions now include a hard rule: stop and flag when the conversation moves from diagnosis to prescription without a commitment. Three months later, his close rate had nearly doubled — not because he got better at pitching, but because he stopped giving the pitch away for free.

Step 4: Create Your First Three Skills

What you’re doing

Package your three most common repeatable tasks as Claude Skills. A Skill loads automatically whenever that task comes up. You stop re-prompting. Claude just knows.

Why this works

Skills use progressive disclosure — only 100 tokens of metadata load at startup, with full instructions activating only when relevant. You can have 10+ Skills without cluttering every conversation. Skills are portable — share them with your team and updates apply instantly.

How to execute

  1. Identify your three most repeated task types. Common choices: sales call prep, follow-up emails, LinkedIn posts, client session summaries, prospect research.
  2. For each Skill, open a new Claude chat and say: 'I want to build a Skill for [task]. Here is what the task involves: [describe it]. Here are examples of good outputs: [paste 1-2 examples]. Please write a SKILL.md file.'
  3. Review the generated SKILL.md. Add anything missing. Remove anything too generic.
  4. Go to Settings > Capabilities > Skills > Upload new Skill. Package the SKILL.md as a ZIP and upload.
  5. Test it: type "/" in any chat to see your Skills. Select and give it a real task.
  6. Save the SKILL.md text in a Google Doc. Update both when you revise.

Pro tip

The fastest way to build a Skill is to do the task manually with Claude first — have a real conversation, get a great output, then say: 'Based on what we just did, write a Skill that automates this process.' Claude extracts the pattern and turns it into reusable instructions.

Step 5: Use Claude in Chrome for Research and YouTube Transcripts

What you’re doing

Install the Claude Chrome extension to bring AI directly into your browser — for competitive research, prospect analysis, and extracting content from YouTube videos without copy-pasting.

Why this works

Claude in Chrome is a full browser agent. It can complete multi-step research tasks that would take 2-3 hours manually in 15-20 minutes. The most practical use case: pulling YouTube transcripts from thought leaders to analyse their positioning and benchmark your approach.

How to execute

  1. Install Claude in Chrome: search 'Claude' in the Chrome Web Store.
  2. For YouTube transcripts: install the Glasp extension alongside. Open any YouTube video, click the Claude icon in the transcript panel.
  3. For a single video: paste the transcript into your Business Coach Project and ask for positioning analysis.
  4. For multiple videos: extract 8-10 transcripts, upload to a dedicated Project. Ask Claude to identify patterns and content gaps.
  5. For prospect research: open their LinkedIn and company site. Ask Claude to build a 1-page brief and suggest the best opening question.

Pro tip

Extract the last 10 YouTube transcripts from a competitor or thought leader you want to track. Ask Claude to identify their positioning strengths, the topics they have not covered, and where your IP fills the gap. Content strategy based on market signals, not guesswork.

How to Improve Your AI Coach Over Time

This is the part most business owners skip. They set up the instructions once and never touch them again. The instructions get stale. The outputs get generic. They blame Claude.

The truth: your AI coach gets better every week if you update it consistently. The update process takes 10 minutes. It compounds faster than almost any other investment you make.

The Weekly Update Loop

  1. Notice: At the end of any session where Claude missed — note what happened. One sentence.
  2. Name it: Which instruction was missing? Profile Preference, Project instruction, or Skill?
  3. Fix it: Add the rule. Be specific: not 'be more direct' but 'lead with the conclusion, then give the reasoning.'
  4. Update: Paste revised instructions into Settings or Project. Test with the same scenario that broke.
  5. Archive: Save every version in a dated Google Doc. You will want to see progress in 3 months.

The Escalation Protocol: Teaching Your Coach to Push Back

One of the most powerful things you can add — and the one most people never configure. This tells Claude what to do when your answers are vague, when you are avoiding a decision, or when you are heading in the wrong direction.

Without an escalation protocol, your AI coach accepts whatever you give it and works with it. That is not a coach. That is an assistant.

A simple three-level escalation protocol:

Level 1 — Vague answer

"That's not specific enough. Give me the actual number / client name / date."

Level 2 — Vagueness persists

"This pattern — avoiding specifics — is costing you. What is the binary outcome you are committing to by [date]?"

Level 3 — Decision avoided

"We are not making progress without a commitment. Make one decision with a 24-hour deadline, or end this session."

Add this to your Project Instructions. Adjust the language to match your style. The result: an AI coach that holds you to the same standard you hold your clients to.

Context Window Management

One practical problem: long conversations reduce quality. Research confirmed that LLMs perform significantly better on information at the start and end of a conversation. Performance can degrade by more than 30% as conversations lengthen.

  • Keep Projects lean: 5-8 core documents. Not your entire Google Drive.
  • Pre-process large documents: Use NotebookLM or Gemini to extract the 80/20 first.
  • Use Skills instead of long prompts: Skills load only when relevant.
  • Start fresh conversations deliberately: A clean slate at 70% context beats a degraded session at 95%.
