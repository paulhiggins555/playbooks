Overview

This playbook turns existing market conversations into positioning content without a content calendar, editorial planning, or dedicated writing blocks.

When your market is already talking about something, you have a 2-4 hour window to capture that attention and redirect it toward your positioning. Signal Surge is a repeatable system for spotting those conversations, scoring whether they are worth your time, finding the counterpoint only you can offer, and publishing fast enough to ride the wave while it is live.

This playbook is not about chasing trends. It is about recognising when a conversation aligns with what you stand for and moving before the moment passes. A single Signal Surge post delivers 4-5x your normal reach, drives story-based comments that feed the algorithm, and reinforces your expert positioning with people who have never heard of you.

The signal is clustering. When three or more people mention the same topic independently within a short window, attention is pooling. That is your window.

Step 3: The Three Hard Questions Before You Build

Confirm all three before proceeding to the 8-step process below.

1. Is there a clear counterpoint?

Signal Surge is not summary content. If the only angle is agreement or recapping what happened, stop. There is no post here.

EXAMPLE

No counterpoint: "Salesforce announced new AI features." (Summary. Skip.)

Clear counterpoint: "Salesforce added 40 AI features this quarter. Your clients will not use 37 of them. The 3 that matter are the ones that change who builds what."

2. Does it connect to Positioning, Productization, or Performance?

If the topic does not touch one of the three Multiplier Method pillars for SaaS consulting partners, skip it regardless of score.

EXAMPLE

No pillar connection: "Elon Musk said something controversial." (Unrelated. Skip.)

Clear pillar connection: "Monday.com restructured its partner tiers." Connects directly to Positioning (WHO should still partner with them) and Performance (how to protect revenue in a restructured tier).

3. Would you say this out loud at a peer founder event?

If the angle sounds like generic content commentary rather than a peer-to-peer insight from someone who has built and sold a consulting business, reframe or skip.

EXAMPLE

Fails the test: "AI is changing everything. Here are 5 things to watch." (Generic. Skip.)

Passes the test: "Three of my NetSuite clients got the same restructuring email last week. The ones who read it as a threat are reacting. The ones who read it as a pricing signal are moving."

If any of the three hard questions is a no, stop. Return the signal to your watchlist and monitor. A signal that fails today may score higher in 72 hours as discussion builds.

Part 2: The 8-Step Signal Surge Process

You have scored your signal at 5 or above. All three hard questions passed. Now execute.

Total time from signal to published post: 45-90 minutes. The faster you move, the more reach you capture. Speed beats optimisation when riding a live signal.

Step 1: Spot the Signal Cluster

Time: 5 minutes

DO THIS: Scan your LinkedIn feed with intention. You are looking for the same topic appearing from at least three independent sources within a short window. One post is noise. Three is a signal.

AI PROMPT

I keep seeing multiple people in my [niche/platform] market talking about [topic].

Help me confirm whether this qualifies as a Signal Cluster worth responding to.

Here is what I have seen so far: [describe 2-3 posts or sources and their engagement levels].

Does this have enough momentum? What is the discussion volume and recency?

What angle would someone with my positioning ([your positioning statement]) bring to this?

YOU WILL GET: Confirmation that a signal cluster exists and initial angle ideas to evaluate.

EXAMPLE

Signal spotted: Jay McBain posts about the Salesforce partner tier overhaul. Nick Johnston shares a breakdown of what changes. Three NetSuite consultants you follow are asking what it means for them.

That is three independent sources, all in your ICP's world, within 48 hours. Score it.

Quick score: Discussion volume = 2 points. Recency = 2 points. ICP relevance = 2 points. Multiplier connection = 2 points. Platform disruption = 2 points. Total: 10/10. Build within 24 hours.

Step 2: Go to the Source

Time: 15-20 minutes

DO THIS: Do not react to reactions. Find the original interview, article, podcast, or post and consume it directly. You are looking for details most people skimming the headlines will never see. The counterpoint lives in those details.

AI PROMPT

I just watched/read/listened to [original source title and creator].

Here is what stood out to me: [your observations - be specific about moments, data points,

or admissions in the source material].

What details here would most people miss?

What is the gap between what this person is saying and what someone who values

[your positioning frame] would say about it?

YOU WILL GET: Source-level observations you can build a counterpoint from. Not a summary. Details.

EXAMPLE

You watch Nick Johnston's full partner program breakdown video (not just the LinkedIn clip).

Most people are reacting to the tier change headline. But at the 14-minute mark, Johnston mentions that the new competency framework rewards "outcome documentation" over certification count.

That detail is your counterpoint. Nobody who skimmed the summary saw it.

Observation: The program is not punishing partners. It is rewarding the ones who can prove client outcomes. That changes the story entirely.

Step 3: Find Your Counterpoint

Time: 10 minutes

DO THIS: Do not summarise. Do not agree. Find the angle that creates productive tension. Your counterpoint should feel slightly unexpected but immediately make sense once someone reads it.

AI PROMPT

Based on my source review and my positioning around [your core positioning],

help me develop a counterpoint to this conversation that:

1. Is not critical or combative toward the original creator

2. Creates curiosity and productive tension

3. Connects to what I stand for

4. Would make someone in my target audience stop scrolling

Give me 3 counterpoint options.

For each one, write the opening line I could use to lead the post.

Flag which one connects most directly to my positioning.

YOU WILL GET: Three counterpoint options with opening lines. You pick one.

EXAMPLE

Counterpoint option 1: "Salesforce did not restructure their partner program. They restructured their tolerance for partners who cannot prove outcomes. Those are different problems."

Counterpoint option 2: "Every Salesforce partner is reading the new tier framework as a threat. The ones who survive will be the ones who read it as a client conversation template."

Counterpoint option 3 (chosen): "The consultants panicking about the Salesforce overhaul are the ones who built their business on certifications. The ones who built it on client outcomes just got a competitive moat."

Option 3 chosen because it connects directly to positioning around the WHO decision and the shift from credentials to outcomes.

Step 4: Write Your Frame

Time: 10 minutes

DO THIS: Connect the counterpoint to your positioning. After reading your post, people should understand your worldview, not just your reaction to this one topic. The frame is what makes the post do positioning work, not just engagement work.

AI PROMPT

I have chosen this counterpoint: [your chosen counterpoint].

Help me connect it to my positioning around [your positioning statement].

Write the Frame section of my post (2-4 sentences) that:

1. Shows why this counterpoint matters to my specific audience

2. Reinforces what I stand for

3. Does NOT explain the solution (that is paid expertise)

4. Creates the gap my audience needs to feel before the CTA lands

YOU WILL GET: A 2-4 sentence positioning frame ready to drop into your post.

EXAMPLE

Frame written:

"The consultants I work with who built on outcomes already had the answer Salesforce is now asking for. The ones scrambling right now built the wrong thing and are trying to retrofit proof onto a business that was never designed to produce it.

That is not a certification problem. That is a positioning problem."

Notice: The frame does not explain how to fix the positioning problem. That is the paid work. The frame just makes the audience feel the gap.

Step 5: Build the Full Post

Time: 10 minutes

DO THIS: Assemble all parts into a text-only LinkedIn post. The hook names the conversation and creates curiosity. The counterpoint delivers the tension. The frame connects to your worldview. The closing question invites stories, not opinions.

AI PROMPT

Build my complete Signal Surge LinkedIn post using these elements:

HOOK (name the conversation, create curiosity): [your draft or ask AI to write it]

COUNTERPOINT: [your chosen counterpoint]

FRAME: [the frame we just built]

CLOSING QUESTION: Write a question that asks people to share their own version

or experience, not their opinion on my point.

Format requirements:

- Text only. No bullet points.

- Paragraphs of 1-3 sentences.

- Total under 250 words.

- Tone: direct, warm, commercially credible.

- No generic phrases: no "game-changing", "navigate", "leverage", or "unlock".

YOU WILL GET: A complete, publish-ready LinkedIn post.

EXAMPLE

Completed post (example):

Salesforce just restructured their entire partner program.

Every consultant I know is reading the same email and asking: "How do we keep our tier?"

The consultants who built their business on certifications are panicking.

The ones who built on client outcomes just got handed a competitive moat.

The new framework does not ask how many badges you have.

It asks what your clients achieved. And whether you can prove it.

That is not a certification problem. That is a positioning problem.

The ones scrambling right now were never set up to answer that question.

When did you first realise your business was built on credentials instead of outcomes?

(224 words. Text only. Closing question invites stories.)

Step 6: Publish Immediately

Time: 5 minutes

DO THIS: Do not wait for a better time. Do not add an image. Do not run it past three people for feedback. Speed matters more than optimisation when riding a signal. The window is 2-4 hours from when the signal cluster peaked.

AI PROMPT

Review my post one final time before I publish: [paste your post].

Check for:

1. Does the hook name the conversation clearly?

2. Is the counterpoint unexpected but immediately logical?

3. Does the frame connect to a worldview, not just an opinion on this topic?

4. Does the closing question invite stories, not debate?

5. Is it under 250 words?

6. Any generic phrases or AI-sounding language to remove?

Flag anything that would make a Salesforce/HubSpot/NetSuite consultant scroll past it.

Then confirm it is ready to publish.

YOU WILL GET: Final confirmation your post is ready. Then publish without delay.

A Signal Surge post published 3 hours after the peak performs 40-60% worse than one published in the window. If you are not ready to publish within the window, save the counterpoint for next time. Do not post a stale signal.

Step 7: Engage in the First Hour

Time: 30-60 minutes

DO THIS: Reply to every comment in the first 60 minutes. Ask follow-up questions. Acknowledge their stories. Do not debate. Do not defend. Every reply extends the algorithmic life of the post and signals to LinkedIn that this content deserves wider distribution.

AI PROMPT

Someone commented on my Signal Surge post with:

[paste the comment]

Help me write a reply that:

1. Acknowledges their specific story or point (not generic praise)

2. Asks one follow-up question to deepen the conversation

3. Does not defend my post or debate their view

4. Stays under 50 words

This person is a [SaaS platform] partner doing approximately [revenue range].

YOU WILL GET: Replies ready to copy-paste directly into LinkedIn comments.

EXAMPLE

Comment received: "This is exactly what happened to us after the HubSpot tier changes two years ago. We had three certifications per consultant and zero client case studies."

Reply written: "That HubSpot moment is the one most people talk about only in retrospect. What did you build after that that changed the pattern? The outcome documentation piece or something else?"

Why this works: It acknowledges their specific story, asks a follow-up that invites more depth, and avoids defending or elaborating on the post itself.

Step 8: Drop a Comment on the Source

Time: 5 minutes

DO THIS: Go to the original interview, article, or post and leave a comment. Acknowledge what they got right, offer your alternative lens, and end with a thought-provoker. No links. No promotion. Just value. The people curious enough to check the comments will find you.

AI PROMPT

Write a comment I can post on the original [interview/article/post] by [creator name].

My counterpoint was: [your counterpoint].

The comment should:

1. Acknowledge what they got right (genuine, one sentence)

2. Offer my alternative lens in one to two sentences

3. End with a thought-provoking question

4. Include zero links and zero promotion

5. Stay under 100 words

YOU WILL GET: A comment ready to post on the source material.

EXAMPLE

Comment posted on Nick Johnston's original video:

"This is the clearest breakdown of the new framework I have seen. The competency shift from certification count to outcome documentation is the part most partners are glossing over.

The partners who built on credentials are reading this as a threat.

The ones who built on client results are reading it as a moat.

Which of those two groups do you think Salesforce is designing for?"

(82 words. No links. No promotion. Ends with a question that invites a real answer.)

What to Avoid

  • Chasing topics outside your positioning. If the conversation does not connect to what you teach or stand for, skip it. Reach without positioning is noise.
  • Summarising. Summary is commodity content. Anyone can do it. A Signal Surge post must offer a perspective people cannot get from reading the original.
  • Attacking the source. Counterpoint is not criticism. You are offering a different lens, not tearing someone down. The goal is tension that invites conversation, not conflict.
  • Waiting for assets. If you stop to create a graphic, you miss the window. Publish text first. Add media only if the post gains significant traction and you want to extend it.
  • Ending with "What do you think?" That invites debate, not stories. Close with a question that asks people to share their own version of the moment.
  • Building on a score below 5. A weak signal wastes 90 minutes for a post that lands flat. Hold it or skip it.

Hook Structures for Signal Surge Posts

Four structures that consistently work for SaaS consulting partner content. Pick one and use it to open your post.

1. Tension / Stake

Name a change and what it puts at risk.

EXAMPLE

"Monday.com just replaced 100 SDRs with AI agents. Most of their implementation partners found out the same way their clients did."

2. Myth Challenge

State what most people believe, then immediately challenge it.

EXAMPLE

"More certifications won't fix a positioning problem. The Salesforce partner list is full of 5-star certified consultants with no pipeline."

3. Pattern Recognition

Name a pattern you are seeing across multiple clients or conversations.

EXAMPLE

"Most Salesforce partners hit $600K and then hit a wall they cannot see. Not a lead problem. Not a delivery problem. A WHO problem."

4. Open Loop

Start a story and stop before the resolution.

EXAMPLE

"There is a reason the best SaaS partners stop talking about the platform. The ones who figured it out are not going to tell you in a LinkedIn post."
