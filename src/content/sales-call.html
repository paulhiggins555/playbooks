Overview

This playbook transforms how your tech consulting business runs sales calls, from pitching features and giving away free consulting, into a structured process that surfaces what the buyer actually cares about and closes deals at $10K to $50K+.

Most tech consultants lose deals before they start. They enter a call in "tell mode," walking prospects through product features, integrations, and methodology. The buyer zones out. Budget objections appear. The deal stalls.

The root problem is not your product. It is that you are selling the GPS, not the destination. Buyers do not care how you build the solution. They care what changes on their P&L when you do.

This playbook gives you a three-stage sales process built specifically for mixed project and retainer deals at the $10K to $50K range. It covers how to qualify before you get on a call, how to run a 30-minute discovery session that uncovers real financial pain, and how to move into a paid scoping engagement that converts into a full project or retainer.

Stop selling what you do. Start selling what they get.

Pre-flight checklist

Before your first call, make sure you have:

  • A pre-qualify form in your scheduler with the 5 questions below (filter before they book)
  • A clear revenue floor for your ICP (e.g., $500K+ annual revenue, at least one employee)
  • Your paid scoping session priced and described (suggest $500 to $1,500 depending on deal size)
  • Two to three client outcome stories with specific numbers (not "we helped them automate" but "they got 8 hours a week back and hired their first ops person")
  • A simple ROI framing for your offer: what P&L line changes when you deliver your solution?
  • A disqualification script ready (so you can end calls cleanly when it is not a fit)
  • Fathom or equivalent recording tool active before every call

Step-by-step process

Step 1: Pre-qualify before they book

What you are doing: Filtering out bad fits before you invest 30 minutes in a call. Anyone who cannot answer these five questions clearly is not ready.

Why this works: The single biggest time drain in tech consulting sales is discovery calls with people who were never going to buy. A pre-qualify form stops 40 to 60% of those calls from happening. The ones who do book are already invested.

How to execute: Add these questions to your booking form (Calendly, Cal.com, or your scheduler):

1. Where are you in your decision process? (Just exploring / Comparing options / Ready to move forward)

2. Who is involved in making this decision? (Just me / Me and one other / A committee)

3. Why now? What has made this a priority? (Open text)

4. What is your rough budget range for this project? (Under $5K / $5K-$15K / $15K-$30K / $30K+)

5. If this succeeded perfectly, what would that look like for your business? (Open text)

Review the answers before every call. If you see "just exploring" and a budget under your minimum, cancel and redirect to your newsletter or a free resource. Do not waste the 30 minutes.

Common mistake: Skipping the form because you want more calls. More unqualified calls does not close the revenue gap. Better calls do.

Pro tip: Add one question that surfaces urgency: "What happens if you do not fix this in the next 6 months?" Answers here will prime your cost of inaction conversation before you even meet.

Step 2: Run the 30-minute discovery call

What you are doing: Diagnosing the prospect's real financial pain. No solutions. No demos. No features. Diagnosis only.

Why this works: Every tech consultant I work with loses deals by solving too early. When you give away the strategy on the discovery call, the prospect either DIYs it or decides they do not need you anymore. Your job here is to surface the gap and make them feel it, not fill it.

How to execute: Follow this structure exactly.

Open (0 to 3 min)

Say: "I reviewed your form and saw you mentioned [PAIN FROM FORM]. Before we get into the business mechanics, what would solving this actually mean for you personally?"

This question shifts the conversation from the technical to the personal. Most buyers have never been asked this. It creates immediate connection and surfaces the real motivation behind the call.

Surface the Business Pain (3 to 12 min)

Say: "Walk me through your week. How much time are you spending on delivery versus working on the business?"

Say: "What is the business generating on its own right now? Where does most of that revenue come from?"

Say: "Most [CLIENT TYPE] I work with are dealing with one of three things: [PAIN 1], [PAIN 2], or [PAIN 3]. Which one is loudest for you?"

Say: "Why now? What has made this a priority this month versus six months ago?"

Say: "Have you tried fixing this before? What happened?"

Let them talk. Your job is to ask, listen, and take notes. Do not offer solutions. Do not share how you would approach it. If they ask directly how you would fix it, say:

"I want to make sure I fully understand your situation before I jump to answers. That would be like a doctor prescribing before they have run the blood tests. Let me ask a couple more questions first."

Quantify the Cost of Inaction (12 to 20 min)

Say: "What is the cost of NOT solving this in the next 6 months, in lost revenue, wasted time, or missed opportunity?"

If they struggle to answer, help them:

Say: "Let me ask it differently. If nothing changes in the next 6 months, what does that cost you? Even roughly."

Get a number. Even a rough one. This becomes the ROI anchor for your scoping session and proposal. Without this number, every pricing conversation becomes a negotiation against your rate rather than a comparison against their loss.

Check the Decision Process (20 to 25 min)

Say: "Who else needs to be comfortable with this decision before it moves forward?"

Say: "How do you typically make investment decisions like this?"

Say: "Is this a sooner thing or a later thing for you?"

Confirm and Close the Discovery (25 to 30 min)

Say: "Let me make sure I have got this right. You want [GOAL] but [OBSTACLE] is in the way. You have tried [PAST ACTION] and it did not work. And you said this is costing you roughly [THEIR NUMBER] if it stays unresolved. Is that accurate?"

Use their exact words. This proves you listened and builds trust before you pitch anything.

If it is a fit, introduce the next step:

Say: "Based on what you have shared, I think I can help. The way I work is we run a paid scoping session before any proposal. It is [PRICE], about 60 minutes, and you walk away with a clear roadmap regardless of what you decide to do next. If you move forward with the full project, the [PRICE] comes off. Does that make sense as a next step?"

Common mistake: Ending the discovery call with "I will send you some information." This kills momentum. Close to the scoping session on the call.

Pro tip: Before the call ends, confirm the cost of inaction number out loud. "So just to confirm, you said the cost of leaving this unresolved is around $[X]/month. That is what we are working against." This cements the ROI frame in their mind.

Step 3: Run the paid scoping session

What you are doing: Delivering a structured 60-minute session where you diagnose their full situation, identify the specific solution, and present a clear next step. This is where you do the real consulting. It is paid.

Why this works: The paid scoping session solves three problems at once. It filters out tyre kickers who are not serious. It covers your time for doing real diagnostic work. And it creates a natural path to the full engagement because you are delivering value before asking for the larger investment.

How to execute:

  1. Recap the discovery call in their words. "Last time you said [PAIN] was costing you roughly [NUMBER] and you wanted [OUTCOME]. Still accurate?"
  2. Go deeper on the business. Review their current process, tools, team structure, and where the specific bottleneck sits.
  3. Translate features to outcomes. For every problem area you identify, name the P&L impact. "This is adding roughly [X] hours/week in manual work at your current team size. That is roughly $[COST] per month in labour."
  4. Present the roadmap. Give them a clear 3 to 4 step approach to fix the problem. This is your consulting. This is what they paid for.
  5. Present the full engagement. "To implement this properly, here is what working together looks like. The investment is [PRICE]. Based on the $[THEIR COST OF INACTION] you mentioned, this pays for itself in [TIMEFRAME]. Ready to move forward?"

Common mistake: Giving the full roadmap for free in the discovery call and then trying to charge for the scoping session. The discovery call is diagnosis only. The roadmap is paid.

Pro tip: Use a live ROI calculator during the scoping session. Even a basic spreadsheet that calculates payback period using their numbers is more persuasive than any pitch. When they see their own numbers produce the result, it stops being your opinion.

Step 4: Present the proposal (30 min)

What you are doing: Confirming the decision, presenting the investment tied to their cost of inaction, and getting a yes or a clear next step. No proposals sent cold. Always live.

Why this works: Proposals sent by email die in inboxes. When you present live, you can handle objections in real time, anchor the investment to their own numbers, and close the same call.

How to execute:

Open: "What has been on your mind since our last conversation? Has anything changed?"

Confirm: "Let me make sure I have this right. You want [GOAL] but [OBSTACLE] is in the way. You tried [PAST ACTION]. This is costing you roughly [NUMBER]. Still accurate?"

Present: Walk through only: what you deliver, how it works at a high level, and what outcome they get. Keep it to 3 things maximum. Tie each one to their stated problem.

Investment: "The investment is [AMOUNT]. You said this problem is costing you roughly [THEIR NUMBER]. Does [AMOUNT] to fix it permanently make sense?"

Surface blockers: "Besides the investment, is there anything else that would prevent you from moving forward?"

Close: "What questions do you have? I will send the agreement now. Can you sign and make the deposit today so we can lock in [START DATE]?"

Common mistake: Sending the proposal and waiting. Follow up on Day 3 by phone, Day 7 with a relevant client result, Day 10 with a direct ask. If no response after 14 days, send the close-your-file email.

Step 5: Handle objections without dropping price

What you are doing: Responding to hesitation in a way that keeps you in the position of the expert, not the vendor chasing a deal.

Why this works: Most objections are not about money. They are about risk. The prospect is not sure they will get the outcome. Your job is to surface the real concern without assuming it is price.

The approach: When you hear an objection, assume the negative first. Make it easy for them to say no. Then let them correct you. This is called the negative reverse.

Budget objection

They say: "It is more than we expected."

You say: "Fair enough. Sounds like the cost of staying where you are is manageable right now. This probably is not urgent enough to justify the investment?"

Wait. If they say yes, ask what would need to change. If they correct you ("No, it is urgent"), ask: "So the investment is the gap, not the urgency. What would the outcome need to be worth for this to make sense?"

Need to think about it

They say: "I need to think it over."

You say: "Fair. When most people tell me they want to think it over, it usually means no but they want to be polite. That is not what is happening here, is it?"

Wait. Let them correct you. If they confirm it is no, ask what would need to change. If they say they are still interested, ask specifically what they need to think through and address it now.

Need to check with someone

They say: "I need to run this past my partner / team."

You say: "Makes sense. Sounds like you are probably not the one who would push this forward anyway. Should I set up a call directly with them?"

Wait. If they say yes, get the contact. If they correct you ("No, I am driving this"), ask: "What do they need to see to feel comfortable? Let us make sure you have those answers before that conversation."

Timing

They say: "Not the right time. Maybe next quarter."

You say: "Fair. Most people who say later are really saying no but do not want to be direct about it. Is that what is happening here?"

Wait. If they confirm, ask what would need to change. If they correct you, ask specifically what needs to happen before they can move forward and set a firm follow-up date.

Weekly execution routine

Before Every Discovery Call (10 min)

  • Review their pre-qualify form answers
  • Look up their LinkedIn or website for one specific observation
  • Translate your solution into P&L language for their specific situation
  • Prepare the cost of inaction question based on their industry

After Every Discovery Call (5 min)

  • Log the cost of inaction number they gave you in your CRM
  • Tag the call outcome: scoping booked / follow-up needed / disqualified
  • Send the scoping session calendar link within 2 hours if they agreed

Weekly Pipeline Review (30 min, every Monday)

  • Review all open opportunities by stage
  • Run the ghosting check: anyone no response for 7+ days gets the check-in script
  • Anyone no response after 14 days gets the close-your-file email
  • Calculate conversion rate for the week: discovery calls held vs scoping sessions booked

Next steps

  1. Today: Add the 5 pre-qualify questions to your booking form. Takes 15 minutes.
  2. This week: Build your feature-to-outcome translation table for your specific solution. Identify the 3 P&L metrics your work changes.
  3. By end of Week 1: Run your next discovery call using the Step 2 framework. Record it in Fathom. Review the transcript after for how many times you gave away a solution before the scoping session.
  4. By end of Month 1: Track your discovery to paid scoping conversion rate. If below 40%, the bottleneck is either the cost of inaction question or you are introducing the scoping session too early (before they have named their own number).


Bonus: review your sales calls with AI

Most tech consultants finish a sales call and move on. The ones who close more deals stop for 10 minutes and ask one question: what did I actually do well and what cost me the deal?

The problem is most people do not know what good looks like. They wing it, they lose deals, and they cannot identify why. This bonus section fixes that with a two-part system: an AI call review you can run yourself, and a direct review from Paul.

Part 1: Set up your AI sales call reviewer

Create a dedicated project in your preferred LLM (Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini). A project saves your instructions permanently so you do not have to re-enter them every time. Then paste in this prompt once as the project instructions.

From then on, every time you want a call reviewed, you paste in the transcript and ask for the table. Takes 5 minutes.

Step 1: Record every call

Use Fathom, Otter.ai, or your preferred tool. Make sure you have permission from the prospect. In most jurisdictions you need to state the call is being recorded at the start.

Step 2: Extract the full transcript

Fathom produces a full transcript automatically. Copy the entire transcript text. Do not summarise it. Paste the raw transcript into your LLM project.

The habit

One call reviewed per month is the minimum. The consultants who improve fastest review every call for the first 90 days, then drop to monthly once the patterns are locked in.

Follow the 70-20-10 rule for sales development: 70% from reviewing and doing real calls, 20% from internal training and frameworks like this playbook, 10% from external courses or coaching.

The goal is not perfection on every call. The goal is to spot the one moment where you gave away the answer before they felt the problem. Fix that one thing and your conversion rate moves.

Your next deal is not lost because of your price. It is lost because the buyer did not feel the cost of staying where they are. This playbook fixes that.
