About this playbook

This playbook moves you from using Claude like a search engine to running it like a business operating system. It is based on how I use Claude every day to run Paul Higgins Mentoring: preparing sales calls, coaching clients, creating content, and managing my team, without working more than 30 hours a week.

It is aimed at an intermediate level. You have used an AI tool before. You have probably asked it to write something, summarise something, or help you think through a problem. You get some value. But you are not running structured systems. You are not getting consistent outputs. And you are re-explaining yourself every single conversation.

This playbook fixes that. By the end, you will have a configured AI environment that knows who you are, what you do, and how you work, and gets better every week without extra effort from you.

The core idea

Claude is not a tool you use. It is a team member you onboard, train, and improve over time. Most SaaS partners skip the onboarding. This playbook does it properly.

Who this is for

  • SaaS partners (e.g. Salesforce, Zoho, Microsoft, Monday, NetSuite, Acumatica) at mid-to-high six figures: implementation firms, automation specialists, systems integrators
  • Solo operators or small teams (1-10 people) who deliver high-value work and need to protect their time
  • People already using Claude or another LLM casually who want to get structured results
  • Business owners who are done re-explaining context every conversation

What this is not

  • A beginner introduction to AI (if you have never used Claude, start at claude.ai first)
  • An advanced automation or agentic AI guide (that comes after this)
  • A generic productivity guide. Every example here is drawn from how I run my own business

Before You Start: What You Need

Complete this checklist before moving to the step-by-step section. Each item is specific. Do not skip.

  • Claude account on a paid plan
    The Pro plan ($20 USD/month) is the minimum. It gives you access to Projects, Skills, and style settings. The Max plan ($100 USD/month) gives you extended context and priority access. It is worth it if you use Claude for 2+ hours daily.
  • A Google account used only for work
    Give your team access to this account to share Projects and files. Keep personal Google separate. This is a quick setup that pays dividends immediately.
  • Fathom or another call recorder active
    Transcripts are your raw content material. You need them before you can use Claude for call prep, case studies, or content. Fathom is free for basic use.
  • 3-5 recent client call transcripts saved
    These go into your first Project as context. Export from Fathom or copy-paste from any recorder. Minimum 1,000 words each.
  • A clear description of your WHO and WHAT
    In 2-3 sentences: who you serve (industry, platform, revenue level) and what you sell them (service, product, or outcome). This goes into your Profile Preferences in Step 1.
  • Claude in Chrome extension installed
    Available free at the Chrome Web Store: search "Claude". Required for the YouTube transcript workflow in Step 5.
  • A note on what you repeat most
    Write down 3 tasks you do every week that feel like copy-paste: same structure, different content. These become your first Skills. Examples: call agendas, follow-up emails, LinkedIn posts, client update summaries.

How to Build Your Claude Operating System

Follow these five steps in order. Each one builds on the last. Do not skip to Skills before you have set up your Profile and your first Project. The sequence matters.

Step 1: Set Your Profile Preferences

What you're doing: Write a clear, specific description of who you are, how you work, and what you need from Claude. This loads at the start of every conversation. It is the foundation of everything.

Why this works: Claude has no memory between sessions by default. Every new chat starts cold. Profile Preferences change that. They load automatically and tell Claude who it is talking to before you say a word. Anthropic's own guidance describes this as telling Claude "who you are and how you work" and recommends treating this like onboarding a new team member. I have updated mine over 30 times. It gets sharper every iteration.

How to execute

  1. Go to Settings (top right) → Profile → find the preferences field.
  2. Write 300-500 words covering: your role and business, your clients and what they need, your communication preferences, specific things Claude should always or never do.
  3. Use this template structure: WHO I AM / HOW I COMMUNICATE / MY CLIENTS / ALWAYS DO / NEVER DO.
  4. Test it: open a new chat, ask Claude to introduce itself to one of your clients. If the response feels off-brand or generic, refine the instructions.
  5. Save a copy of your preferences in a Google Doc. You will update this often.

Common mistakes

  • Writing it like a CV: listing credentials instead of giving Claude behavioural rules. Claude does not need your backstory. It needs instructions.
  • Making it too long. 500 words maximum. Every line should change how Claude responds. If removing it would change nothing, cut it.

Pro tip

The most impactful lines are the "Never do" ones. Telling Claude what to stop doing (never start with "Great question!", never use bullet points when I ask for prose, never summarise what I just said back to me) eliminates the default annoying behaviours faster than any positive instruction.

Step 2: Configure Your Style

What you're doing: Create a custom style that matches your voice and communication format. This is separate from preferences. It controls how Claude presents information, not what it knows about you.

Why this works: Claude offers four preset styles (Normal, Concise, Formal, Explanatory) but the real power is in custom styles. You can upload samples of your own writing and Claude will analyse your voice and generate a matching style. For business owners with a distinct communication style, especially those producing LinkedIn content or client-facing documents, this is worth 20 minutes.

How to execute

  1. Go to Settings → Styles → Create new style.
  2. Option A: Upload 3-5 examples of your best writing (emails, LinkedIn posts, proposals). Claude analyses the samples and generates a matching style.
  3. Option B: Describe your style in text (e.g., "Short sentences. Commercially direct. No bullet points in emails. Peer-to-peer tone. Never start with compliments.").
  4. Name it something clear (e.g., "Paul Voice" or "Client Comms").
  5. Set it as your default or select it at the start of relevant conversations.
  6. Test with a real task: ask Claude to draft a follow-up email to a prospect. Compare with and without the style applied.

Common mistakes

  • Using the preset styles as a substitute. "Concise" just makes responses shorter. It does not match your specific tone.
  • Setting and forgetting. Your style should evolve as your voice does. Review it every 6-8 weeks.

Pro tip

If you write LinkedIn content, build a LinkedIn-specific style separate from your email style. The tone for a post (hook-driven, short lines, direct) is different from the tone for a proposal (measured, specific, structured). Switching between them takes 5 seconds.

Step 3: Build Your First Project: The AI Business Coach

What you're doing: Create a dedicated Claude Project that acts as your AI business advisor, loaded with context about your business, your clients, and your methodology. This is the equivalent of hiring a senior strategist who has read every document in your business.

Why this works: Projects give Claude persistent context. Conversations inside the project draw on uploaded documents and custom instructions that you set once and never repeat. Anthropic confirms that Projects with RAG can hold up to 10x more content than a standard conversation. I use a project called BREN as my core business coach. It has my methodology, client results, and sales frameworks loaded. When I drop in a call transcript and ask for feedback, it knows what good looks like because it has seen 200+ client patterns.

How to execute

  1. Go to claude.ai → Projects → New Project.
  2. Name it clearly: "Business Coach" or your business name.
  3. Write Project Instructions (600-1,000 words). Cover: what this project is for, your business model and methodology, rules for how Claude should respond, what Claude should always and never do in this context.
  4. Upload your core knowledge files: your niche description, your offer stack, 3-5 call transcripts, any frameworks or methodologies you use with clients.
  5. Test with a real question: "I have a prospect who [describe situation]. What should my approach be on the call?" Claude should give specific, context-aware advice.
  6. If the response is generic, your instructions need more specificity. Add concrete examples of what good advice looks like in your context.

Common mistakes

  • Uploading too many files at once. Start with 5-8 core documents. Research shows that more documents in the context window can reduce quality. The "lost in the middle" effect means Claude pays less attention to information buried in large knowledge bases. Keep it lean.
  • Writing instructions as an essay instead of a decision framework. Claude follows rules better than prose. Use clear directives.

Pro tip

Use the Project Instructions to tell Claude how to handle things you get wrong regularly. A consultant I work with kept giving away strategy before the prospect had committed. His AI coach instructions now include a hard rule: stop and flag when the conversation moves from diagnosis to prescription without a commitment. The AI catches what he misses at the moment.


Step 4: Create Your First Three Skills

What you're doing: Package your three most common repeatable tasks as Claude Skills. A Skill is a set of instructions Claude loads automatically whenever that task comes up. You stop re-prompting. Claude just knows.

Why this works: Skills are Anthropic's answer to the "I keep typing the same prompt" problem. According to Anthropic's official guidance, Skills use progressive disclosure: only 100 tokens of metadata load at startup, with full instructions activating only when relevant. This means you can have 10+ Skills available without cluttering every conversation. More importantly, Skills are portable. Once built, you can share them with your team and updates apply instantly.

How to execute

  1. Identify your three most repeated task types. Common choices: sales call prep, follow-up emails, LinkedIn posts, client session summaries, prospect research, weekly team updates.
  2. For each Skill, open a new Claude chat and say: "I want to build a Skill for [task]. Here is what the task involves: [describe it]. Here are examples of good outputs: [paste 1-2 examples]. Please write a SKILL.md file for this."
  3. Review the generated SKILL.md. Add anything missing. Remove anything too generic.
  4. Go to Settings → Capabilities → Skills → Upload new Skill. Package the SKILL.md as a ZIP and upload.
  5. Test it: type "/" in any chat to see your Skills listed. Select it and give it a real task.
  6. Save the raw SKILL.md text in a Google Doc. When you update the Skill, update both the doc and the uploaded version.

Common mistakes

  • Building Skills before you have a Project. Skills work best when paired with a Project that provides the business context. A Skill without context produces generic outputs.
  • Making one Skill do too many things. A "content creation" Skill that handles LinkedIn posts, newsletters, AND YouTube scripts will do all three badly. Separate Skills compose better than one monolithic Skill.

Pro tip

The fastest way to build a Skill is to do the task manually with Claude first. Have a real conversation, get a great output, then say: "Based on what we just did, write a Skill that automates this process for any future use." Claude will extract the pattern from the conversation and turn it into reusable instructions.

Step 5: Use Claude in Chrome for Research and YouTube Transcripts

What you're doing: Install the Claude Chrome extension to bring AI directly into your browser for competitive research, prospect analysis, and extracting content from YouTube videos without copy-pasting.

Why this works: Claude in Chrome is a full browser agent, not a passive sidebar. It can read web pages, navigate between sites, extract structured data, and complete multi-step research tasks that would take 2-3 hours manually in 15-20 minutes. The most practical use case for SaaS partners: pulling YouTube transcripts from thought leaders or prospects to analyse their positioning, extract content ideas, and benchmark your approach against what is working in your market.

How to execute

  1. Install Claude in Chrome: search "Claude" in the Chrome Web Store.
  2. For YouTube transcript extraction: install the Glasp extension alongside Claude in Chrome ("YouTube Summary with Claude"). Open any YouTube video, click the Claude icon in the transcript panel. It opens a Claude tab with the full transcript preloaded.
  3. For a single video: paste the transcript into your Business Coach Project and ask: "What is this person's positioning? What problems do they address? What would I do differently in my niche?"
  4. For multiple videos: extract 8-10 transcripts from the same creator or topic area. Upload them all to a dedicated Claude Project. Ask Claude to identify patterns, common objections, and content gaps.
  5. For prospect research before a sales call: open their LinkedIn, their company site, and any relevant YouTube or podcast content. Ask Claude in Chrome to "build me a 1-page brief on this company: their current challenge, recent activity, and the most relevant question I could open with."
  6. For web page extraction into your main Claude conversation: open the page, copy the key content, paste directly into Claude. The manual method is fast enough for most use cases.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting Claude in Chrome to replace your workflow immediately. It works best on familiar, text-heavy sites: research, LinkedIn, documentation. It is slower than manual for simple tasks you can do in 30 seconds.
  • Using it on financial or sensitive platforms. Claude in Chrome has read access to your logged-in sessions. Keep it away from banking, CRM with client contracts, or anything sensitive.

Pro tip

The highest-ROI use: extract the last 10 YouTube transcripts from a competitor or industry thought leader you want to track. Upload to a Project. Ask Claude to identify their positioning strengths, the topics they have not covered, and where your IP fills the gap. This is a Signal Surge: a content strategy based on market signals, not guesswork.

What Changed When Partners Ran This for 90 Days

Everything above is the setup. This section is what I learned watching SaaS partners in my group run it every week, and the fixes we made along the way. Most of it came from screen-share sessions where the setup looked right on paper and still broke in practice.

Run everything from one home Project

The biggest fix was not a new skill. It was stopping the sprawl. One partner had dozens of loose chats and could never find the conversation he needed. Claude rereads the whole chat every time you ask a question, so long, mixed chats get slow and start to lose the thread.

  • Make it the home. Your Business Coach Project is where every chat and every skill runs. Each task starts with your instructions and files already loaded.
  • One chat per prospect or client. Rename the chat with their name the moment you start it. Claude's chat search is weak, but a named chat inside the Project comes up the second you type the name.
  • One topic per chat. When the topic is done, close it out and start the next one fresh.
  • Work in the browser if anyone else needs to see it. A session that runs locally in the desktop app stays on that computer. If your team or your phone needs the work, use claude.ai.

Consolidate before you close a chat

At the end of any long chat, run a consolidate skill. It writes the decisions, file names, document IDs and next steps from the chat into a Google Doc in your Drive. That does two things. Your chats stay short, which keeps output quality high. And the IP you build lives in your Drive, not only inside Claude, so you keep it whatever platform you use next year.

When you need it again, paste the doc link into a new chat inside the Project and say: "Get this document. Ask me one question at a time to help me prepare for [objective]."

Point instructions at document IDs, not file names

Every Google Doc has an ID: the string in its URL after /d/. Put that ID in your Project instructions and Claude finds the document even after you rename it. Keep the documents you and your coach both edit in one shared folder, so an update made by either of you is the version Claude reads.

People want folders. Claude does not care about them. Archive anything you no longer use, and ask Claude to find a file instead of browsing for it.

Write the message yourself, then let Claude check it

For months my outreach ran the other way. Claude drafted, I edited, and the messages stopped sounding like me. So I flipped it. I spend two minutes on the prospect's profile, write the message the way I would naturally say it (usually by voice), and a critique skill checks it against my rules. You know your market better than any model does. Let Claude catch what you missed, not write what you would say.

The skill chain that runs inside the Project:

  • Client selection skill. Scores each prospect against your ideal client and creates a prospect file. One partner ran it over a 1,100-name Sales Navigator export and cut it to about 200 genuine fits.
  • Outreach critique skill. Reads your draft against your message rules and names the rule you broke.
  • Message log skill. Saves the before, the after and the reason for every change into a messages file. The critique skill reads that file, so within a few weeks it knows how you talk.
  • Consolidate skill. Closes out the chat and files the decisions in your Drive.

When a skill gets it wrong, tell it why in the chat, then ask it to rewrite the skill. I have lost count of the versions mine have been through. That is the point.

Spend the effort after they accept

Send connection requests with no note. Across my own outreach and every outreach specialist I have spoken to, acceptance is the same or better without one, and it frees your time for the message that matters: the first one after they accept.

Work warm to cold. Start with your first connections and current clients, then your CRM, people who engage with your posts, recent profile viewers, trigger events, and vendor reps who already know your ideal clients. Strangers in Sales Navigator are step 13 of 14 in my process. Bought lists come last.

In Sales Navigator, start with an account list of around 1,000 companies, then find the roles you sell to inside them. Never send messages from the Sales Navigator inbox. Replies land in a second inbox you will forget to check.

Judge instructions by the output, not by reading them

When you inherit a skill or a set of instructions, do not read every line. Run it, judge the output, and change only what matters. Not every annoyance earns an edit. Ask whether this is one to fix now or one to let through. To adapt a shared skill, paste it into your Project and say: "Make this specific to me. Ask me one question at a time."

How to Improve Your AI Coach Over Time

This is the part most business owners skip. They set up the instructions once and never touch them again. The instructions get stale. The outputs get generic. They blame Claude.

The truth: your AI coach gets better every week if you update it consistently. The update process takes 10 minutes. It compounds faster than almost any other investment you make.

The weekly update loop

  1. Notice: At the end of any session where Claude missed, repeated itself, or gave you something generic, note what happened. One sentence. "Claude kept using bullet points when I asked for prose." "Claude didn't flag that I was giving free advice."
  2. Name it: Identify which instruction was missing or wrong. Is it a Profile Preference issue (how Claude talks to me in general)? A Project instruction issue (how Claude behaves in this context)? A Skill issue (how Claude executes this specific task)?
  3. Fix it: Open the relevant document (Profile, Project instructions, or Skill SKILL.md). Add the rule or clarification. Be specific: not "be more direct" but "lead with the conclusion, then give the reasoning."
  4. Update: Paste the revised instructions into the Settings or Project. Test with the same scenario that broke. If it works, you are done.
  5. Archive: Save every version of your instructions in a dated Google Doc. You will want to see how far it has come in 3 months.

Real example from a client session

A consultant I work with kept talking too much in discovery calls, giving away strategy before the prospect had committed. We added one line to his AI coach instructions: "If the conversation shifts from diagnosis to prescription, stop and flag it." Now, when he uses his AI coach to prep for a call, it reminds him where the line is. The AI catches the pattern he has been repeating for years.

Context window management: keeping Claude sharp

One practical problem you will hit as you use Claude more: long conversations reduce quality. Research from Stanford and UC Berkeley confirmed that LLMs perform significantly better on information at the start and end of a conversation. Information in the middle gets missed. Performance can degrade by more than 30% as conversations lengthen.

There are four practical responses to this:

  • Keep Projects lean: Upload only what Claude needs for this project, not your entire Google Drive. 5-8 core documents beats 25 documents every time.
  • Pre-process large documents: For heavy research or long transcripts, use Google's NotebookLM or Gemini to extract the 80/20 first. Paste the distilled summary into Claude rather than the full document.
  • Use Skills instead of long prompts: Skills load only when relevant. They do not consume context sitting idle. Replace long repeated prompts with Skills and you immediately free up space for the actual work.
  • Start fresh conversations deliberately: When you notice quality slipping, start a new chat. Paste a 3-sentence summary of context. A clean slate at 70% context is better than a degraded session at 95%.

Ready-to-Use Templates

Template 1: Profile Preferences

When to use: Paste into Settings > Profile and fill in the [brackets]. This is a starting structure. Personalise every line.

WHO I AM

I am [name], founder of [business name]. I help [who you serve] with [what you solve].

My clients are [describe: industry, platform, revenue level]. I work [solo / with a small team].

HOW I COMMUNICATE

I prefer: short paragraphs, direct language, one idea per message.

I communicate in [North American English / Australian English / etc.].

My communication style is commercially warm: peer to peer, not teacher to student.

MY CLIENTS

The people I work with are [describe mindset and sophistication level].

They value results over process. They are skeptical of generic advice.

Use their language: [list 3-5 terms or phrases from your niche].

ALWAYS DO

- Ask me one question at a time when you need more information

- Lead with the conclusion before the reasoning

- Flag when I am about to give away advice I should be charging for

- Use specific numbers, not round estimates

NEVER DO

- Start with "Great question!" or any version of a compliment

- Use bullet points unless I explicitly ask for them

- Give me generic best practices when you have context-specific information available

- Summarise what I just said back to me before answering

Why this works

The "Always/Never" structure gives Claude the clearest possible behavioural rules. Negative instructions (Never) are more impactful than positive ones. They eliminate the default annoying behaviours that erode trust in AI outputs.

Template 2: Project Instructions for an AI Business Coach

When to use: Paste into your Project Instructions field. Adjust all [brackets] before saving.

WHAT THIS PROJECT IS FOR

This is my AI business coach. It helps me prepare for sales calls, review client sessions,

make strategic decisions, and improve my business model.

It does NOT do execution work. It does thinking and decision work.

MY BUSINESS

[Paste your 2-3 sentence WHO and WHAT description here.]

My core methodology is [describe your approach in 2-3 sentences].

My clients typically start at [revenue/situation] and reach [outcome] within [timeframe].

PROOF CLIENTS (reference these in relevant contexts)

- [Client 1 name]: [Specific result with numbers]

- [Client 2 name]: [Specific result with numbers]

RULES FOR THIS PROJECT

- Always start with a binary decision or a specific question, never open-ended exploration

- If I ask about HOW (systems, tools, processes) before WHO and WHAT are confirmed, redirect me

- Flag the Premature Coaching Trap: if I am about to give strategic advice for free, say STOP

- Every recommendation should reference a specific client example where relevant

- Never give generic consulting advice when context-specific guidance is available

DO NOT

- Give me a list of options without recommending one

- Ask multiple questions in a single response

- Use consultant jargon: no "leverage", "synergy", "align", "robust"

- Agree with me just because I pushed back. If you think I am wrong, say so directly

Why this works

The "Rules" and "Do Not" sections are where most business owners stop short. They write context but skip the behavioural rules. The rules are what make the difference between a generic AI and an AI that actually thinks like your best advisor.

Template 3: Skill Creation Prompt

When to use: Paste into a fresh Claude chat to build any new Skill conversationally.

I want to build a Claude Skill for [task name].

Here is what this task involves:

[Describe the task in 3-5 sentences. What triggers it? What is the input? What is the output?]

Here are 2 examples of good outputs for this task:

Example 1: [Paste a real example]

Example 2: [Paste a real example]

Rules this Skill must follow:

- [Rule 1: specific formatting or tone requirement]

- [Rule 2: what the Skill should always include]

- [Rule 3: what the Skill should never do]

Please write a complete SKILL.md file I can upload to Claude.

Include: YAML frontmatter with name and description, and clear step-by-step instructions.

Why this works

The two examples are the most important part. Claude learns from patterns. Giving it a good output and a great output allows it to understand the range and calibrate the Skill accordingly. Without examples, Skills produce generic outputs.

Template 4: YouTube Transcript Analysis Prompt

When to use: After extracting a transcript using the Glasp extension, paste this prompt into your Business Coach Project.

Here is the transcript from [creator name]'s video: [paste title and URL]

[Paste full transcript]

Please analyse this and tell me:

1. Their core positioning: who they serve and what promise they make

2. The specific problems they address most often

3. The frameworks or methodologies they use (named or implied)

4. The objections they preempt or handle

5. Content gaps: topics they do not address that my audience needs

6. One question I could use to open a conversation with someone who follows this creator

Keep this analysis tight: one paragraph per point.

Why this works

The final question (opening a conversation with a follower) is the commercial payoff. You are not just researching content. You are building a bridge to a sales conversation. Every research session should end with a usable outreach angle.

Template 5: Weekly Instruction Review Prompt

When to use: Run this at the end of every week inside your Business Coach Project.

It is the end of the week. I want to update my AI coach instructions based on what I noticed this week.

Three things that did not work as expected this week:

1. [Describe what happened]

2. [Describe what happened]

3. [Describe what happened]

Here are my current Project instructions:

[Paste your current instructions]

Please:

1. Identify which instruction is missing or unclear for each of the three issues above

2. Write specific replacement lines or new additions

3. Flag anything in my current instructions that is vague or contradictory

4. Give me the full revised instructions, ready to copy-paste

Why this works

This prompt uses Claude to improve Claude. The AI helps you refine the instructions that govern its own behaviour. It is faster than editing manually and surfaces blind spots you would not catch yourself.
