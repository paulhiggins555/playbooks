Purpose

Revenue forecasting keeps you focused on what matters: sales. This isn't about tracking cash in the bank—it's about tracking deals closed and momentum building.

Every group call, you'll report one number: your percentage to monthly target. This forces honesty. You're either on track or you're not.

Setting your annual baseline

Step 1: Review Last 12 Months

Pull your revenue by month for the past year. Look for:

  • Average monthly revenue: What's your baseline?
  • Seasonal patterns: Do you have slow months (December, summer)?
  • Growth rate: Are you trending up, flat, or down?

Step 2: Set Your Annual Target

Your annual target should be a stretch but realistic.

Stretch = Makes you uncomfortable but achievable with focused effort Unrealistic = Requires miracles or resources you don't have

Example: If you did $360K last year, a 20-30% increase ($432K-$468K) is stretch. A 100% increase ($720K) without major changes to your model is fantasy.

What counts as revenue

Revenue Recognition: Accrual Basis

Count revenue when the deal closes, not when cash hits the bank.

Example 1: Long-Term Contract

You close a $300K deal on January 15th to be delivered over 10 months.

January gets credited: $30K (full monthly amount) February-October get credited: $30K each month

The deal closed in January, so January gets the first full month of recognition.

Example 2: Retainer Client

You sign a client on January 20th for $5K/month ongoing.

January gets credited: $5K Each subsequent month: $5K until they churn

Example 3: One-Time Project

You close a $45K project on January 10th to be delivered in 90 days.

January gets credited: $15K February gets credited: $15K
March gets credited: $15K

Critical Rule: Deals closed this month count toward this month's target, regardless of when delivery happens or cash arrives.

What NOT to Count

❌ Proposals sent but not closed - Even if they're "90% sure" ❌ Pipeline opportunities - Until signed, it's not revenue ❌ Past revenue from existing contracts - Only count new deals closed this month

The only exception: If you're tracking total MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue), include existing retainers + new deals closed.

Revenue = volume × rate

Before you assume you need 10 new clients to hit target, remember this formula:

Revenue = Number of Clients × Average Deal Size

You have two levers:

  • Volume: More clients
  • Rate: Higher prices

Example:

  • Current: 10 clients × $3K/month = $30K MRR
  • Target: $50K MRR

Option A (Volume): Add 7 new clients at $3K = 17 clients × $3K Option B (Rate): Raise prices to $5K = 10 clients × $5K
Option C (Both): Add 2 clients + raise to $4K = 12 clients × $4K

Most consultants default to Option A (hustle harder). The smart play is often Option B or C (charge more).

Question to ask yourself: Am I underpriced? Could I serve fewer clients at higher rates and make more money?

Weekly tracking & reporting

During the Month

Every week, update your forecast:

Week 1: "I'm at 20% of monthly target" Week 2: "I'm at 45% of monthly target"
Week 3: "I'm at 70% of monthly target" Week 4: "I'm at 95% of monthly target"

This is based on:

  • Deals already closed this month (confirmed revenue)
  • Deals likely to close before month-end (weighted by probability)

Example:

  • Monthly target: $40K
  • Deals closed: $18K
  • Deal in final negotiation (80% likely): $10K × 80% = $8K
  • Current forecast: $18K + $8K = $26K = 65% of target

On Group Calls

You'll report one number:

"I'm forecasting [X]% of my monthly target."

That's it. No excuses, no explanations unless asked.

If you're at 40% in Week 3, the gap is obvious. We then focus on: What needs to happen in the next 7 days to close that gap?

Reminders & best practices

1. This Is Your Best Estimate

Don't stress about perfection. Budget forecasting is:

  • 70% historical data
  • 20% informed assumptions
  • 10% educated guessing

We'll adjust as we work together. The goal is directional accuracy, not precision.

2. We WILL Increase Your Revenue

One thing is certain: Working together will increase your revenue. Your forecast will improve as you implement the Multiplier Method.

First 90 days: Expect messy data and missed targets as you learn the system Months 4-6: Forecasts become more accurate as you build pipeline discipline Months 7+: You'll predict within 10-15% consistently

3. Update Monthly

Review and adjust your targets monthly:

  • Actuals vs. Forecast: How close were you?
  • What caused variance: Pipeline too optimistic? Deals delayed?
  • Adjust next month: Refine assumptions based on what you learned

4. Separate Strategic from Tactical

Use your budget to filter decisions:

Ask: "Does this get me closer to my revenue target THIS MONTH?"

  • If YES → Prioritize
  • If NO → Defer or delegate

Example: Spending 3 hours building a new service offering when you're at 50% of target in Week 3? That's a NO. Close the gap first.

5. Track Leading Indicators

Revenue is a lagging indicator (it already happened). Track these leading indicators weekly:

  • Outreach volume: Calls/emails to qualified prospects
  • Discovery calls booked: Sales conversations scheduled
  • Proposals sent: Opportunities in final stage
  • Close rate: % of proposals that convert

If outreach drops, revenue will drop 30-60 days later. Fix it now.

Final thoughts

  • Honesty: No hiding from the numbers
