Purpose

The newsletter is the nurture channel for PHM. It converts readers who are not yet ready to book into readers who eventually reply and do. Every issue exists to generate one of two outcomes — a reply from a qualified SaaS partner, or a booking at talkpaul.com. Not opens. Not clicks. Replies and bookings.

Who you are writing for

SaaS partners running established founder-led consultancies. Platforms include Salesforce, Zoho, HubSpot, Monday.com, Microsoft, NetSuite, Acumatica, and others. Revenue $200K to $1M USD, sweet spot $300K to $1M. Team of 1 to 10. Primarily US West Coast and Canada, some AU/NZ. Three or more years in operation.

The three problems they show up with:

  • WHO: serving everyone, no clear niche, inconsistent revenue.
  • WHAT: half recurring, half project, dependent on platform channel or referrals.
  • HOW: buried in delivery, can't do outreach, still last set of eyes on everything.

Do not write for solo operators, people who just left corporate, or anyone who wants validation rather than decisions.

The Multiplier Method

Every newsletter sits inside one of three decisions. Name the decision before writing. Never explain the full methodology — show the result, name the pattern, create the gap.

  • WHO: get clear on who you serve. When WHO is clear, marketing sharpens, pricing strengthens, and the right clients find you. Until WHO is clear, nothing else compounds.
  • WHAT: decide what you sell them. The WHAT decision builds a second pillar — a productized service or an AI capability the platform cannot deliver. Something that earns without the owner in every delivery.
  • HOW: build the system so the business does not run through you. Part one: create the capacity through SOPs and AI delegation. Part two: own lead generation. Outreach first, then strategic partners, then content.

The newsletter structure

Every issue has seven sections in this order. The sequence does not change.

  1. Personal opening. Paul writes this on Monday. Two to three sentences about what happened in his week. This is the human entry point. It creates personal connection before the reader reaches any content. Not AI generated.
  2. Three bullet points. Preview the three main pieces of value in the issue. Pulled directly from what follows. Specific not generic. Gives the reader a reason to keep going.
  3. Authority content. One insight or story from a podcast guest or group call. Tied commercially to the ICP's world — why it matters to a SaaS partner right now. Guest's own words where possible. Source must be a transcript.
  4. AI Advantage. Paul writes this. Focus is on AI for revenue generation — marketing and sales — not delivery and operations. Paul draws from his own experience and what he is reading and watching that week. Not AI generated.
  5. Client example. Called Let's Get Real. One client. First name, business type, specific numbers. Show the situation before the decision, the decision made, and what happened next. Source must be a recorded transcript. Check PHM Testimonials before use. Tease the solution, never teach it.
  6. What You Missed. Pulled from comments and questions generated by last week's LinkedIn posts. Invites the reader to participate. Keeps the newsletter connected to the LinkedIn conversation happening that week.
  7. CTA. One call. No framework. Just a straight read on where the real constraint is. Book here: talkpaul.com. This block does not vary.

The proof standard

Client proof is maintained in the PHM Testimonials document. Before using any client story in the client example section, check that document for recorded status, numbers, and approved use. Do not use recalled proof or assume recorded without checking. No recorded proof means no client example. Results only, never the method.

The content loop

The newsletter does not operate in isolation. The authority content section should reinforce the Wednesday LinkedIn post theme where possible. The client example should reinforce the Friday LinkedIn post. This creates a content loop across the week — the reader sees the theme on LinkedIn and then goes deeper in the newsletter. Mel coordinates this when building the weekly content plan.

The subject line

The subject line comes from the client example or the authority content — whichever is stronger that week. Specific result or pattern. No generic claims. No em-dashes. The subject line earns the open the same way the LinkedIn hook earns the click on see more.

Test: would a SaaS partner at $500K revenue stop scrolling their inbox for this line?

Ownership by section

  • Paul writes: personal opening and AI Advantage. Both go into PHM CONTENT 2026 on Monday Australia time.
  • Mel drafts: bullet point preview, authority content, client example, and What You Missed. All go into PHM CONTENT 2026 for Paul's Monday review.

Voice rules

Short sentences. One idea per line where it creates emphasis. No em-dashes. No AI padding phrases — no game-changing, unlock, transform, leverage, in today's landscape, insights, learnings. No fake compliments. Specific over general — names, numbers, timeframes. North American Neutral tone, warm but commercially firm. Results not method. First person always. Double space between paragraphs.

What success looks like

Replies from qualified SaaS partners who recognise themselves in the client example. Bookings at talkpaul.com from readers who have been on the list for weeks or months. Growing open rate from a shrinking but more qualified list. The newsletter is not measured on subscriber count — it is measured on conversations started.
